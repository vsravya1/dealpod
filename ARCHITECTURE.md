# DealPod architecture

The normal purchase path uses published offers: buyer search → confirm requirements → compare availability and tradeoffs → accept agreement → group threshold → individual PayPal sandbox approval and server capture verification → seller-recorded demo shipment.

The unmet-request path is separate: buyers explicitly share confirmed unmet requests with must-haves and flexible preferences → worker pools distinct compatible buyers against one seller SKU and additional inventory → one seller AI assessment plus a separate buyer AI assessment for every participant → seller approves or declines one shared proposal → publication allocates stock → authorized buyer agents join one unit each → buyers approve individual PayPal sandbox payment. Buyers without permission review and join manually.

## Agents and rules

OpenAI Responses structured outputs interpret shopping requirements and generate a seller assessment for the pooled requirements and individual buyer assessments. The buyer and seller assessments are actual API calls. Price, stock quantity, product specifications, group target and cancellation policy come from seller rules and catalog records; AI cannot invent or change them. Sellers may authorize a base quantity price plus larger-group tiers with strictly lower prices. The worker selects the cheapest tier currently supported by enough distinct compatible buyers, never reprices an accepted deal, and never exceeds declared stock. Additional stock is an explicit demo seller declaration, not imported warehouse inventory. One seller SKU policy is supported per seller. The worker waits for at least the declared minimum number of distinct buyers. All grouped buyers must accept one exact configuration; explicit flexible preferences may differ, but hard requirements are never relaxed. The group draft allocates the number of compatible participants, bounded by declared stock, at the authorized quantity price; this is bounded proposal preparation, not autonomous negotiation.

A five-second in-process worker runs while the Node server is running. It processes one job at a time, persists request stages and actor-labelled activity in SQLite, and never publishes or pays automatically. Only prior buyer authorization permits automatic joining after seller publication. Failed AI calls require explicit retry. Restarted jobs become failed and can be retried. Changing requirements blocks publication of an obsolete draft. Seller approval transactionally allocates additional stock and inserts the published offer. Publication, stock allocation, auto-joined commitments, request completion and authorization consumption are committed in one SQLite transaction. Duplicate publication cannot allocate stock or join twice.

Buyers only see their requests. Seller proposal screens receive requirements and assessment records, not payment credentials or chat histories. Profile dropdowns are demonstration controls, not authentication. All supplier offers use one sandbox merchant; there is no escrow or atomic group payment. PayPal capture verification binds the amount, currency and commitment. Seeded participants in legacy groups have no payment records; newly generated deals start at 0 buyers.

Demo fulfillment records seller shipment and dispatched inventory, without booking a carrier. Seller-approved unpaid cancellations release places and promote the FIFO waitlist; existing checkouts or paid purchases require reconciliation/refund handling.

Legacy per-buyer seller review endpoints remain compatible but are not required for published offers. No retrospective AI activity is fabricated.

## Auto-join authority
Auto-join defaults off. A buyer explicitly selects sellers and accepts their displayed agreement version, cancellation policy, US region and USD currency for one unit within a hard budget, group size and 1–7 day expiry. Terms snapshots must still match at publication. Unused permission can be revoked. Expired or revoked permission, stale request revisions, changed terms, unverified required preferences or timing/location, full groups and existing matching commitments pause joining. Auto-join never creates a waitlist or payment. Marking a preference flexible is explicit and remains recorded. Normal manual agreement and PayPal approval remain available.

## Group payments and deadlines
Shipment is gated on the group target, every required recorded buyer payment, and resolved cancellation requests. Payment summaries distinguish buyer payments from legacy prefilled demo places. Newly prepared deals disclose a 24-hour payment window starting when commitments reach the target; one deadline persists across cancellations and waitlist promotions. Existing deals and commitments receive no retroactive payment deadlines or price changes. Overdue unpaid groups require seller resolution; new payment attempts and shipment are blocked, while completed PayPal captures can still be reconciled. No automatic refund, cancellation or replacement occurs on timeout.

## Buyer guidance
The assistant explains which requirements or availability constraints block a request, using current catalog facts. An explicit buyer action can make captured brand, RAM or storage preferences flexible; budget is always retained. This revises the confirmed request and invalidates earlier proposals and authorizations. Unsupported preferences and timing remain disclosed.
